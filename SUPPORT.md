# Support

**Means+Measures**

**Equity of means. Equality of measures.**

Support should make systems easier to understand, use, and improve. People seeking assistance should not need specialized knowledge, institutional connections, or familiarity with development workflows simply to identify a problem or ask a question.

This document explains how to obtain assistance, report problems, request accessibility support, and direct sensitive concerns to an appropriate private channel.

## 1. Finding the right support channel

Means+Measures uses GitHub's collaboration tools to organize project-specific questions, reports, and requests.

| Need | Recommended channel |
|---|---|
| Question about a project or its functionality | Repository Discussions, where enabled |
| Bug or unexpected behavior | Repository Issues |
| Feature or improvement request | Repository Issues or Discussions |
| Documentation question or correction | Repository Issues or pull request |
| Accessibility barrier or accommodation request | Repository Issues, or private email when appropriate |
| Contribution or development assistance | Repository Discussions or [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) |
| Security vulnerability | Private vulnerability reporting or [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) |
| Confidential concern or conduct complaint | [Private contact](mailto:privacy-contact@mail.meansandmeasures.com) |

When a repository provides more specific support instructions, follow those instructions for that project.

## 2. Getting help

### Ask a question

1. Open the relevant Means+Measures repository.
2. Read its README and available documentation for project-specific guidance.
3. Check existing Discussions and Issues for related questions or known problems.
4. Open a new Discussion, if enabled, or use an available support issue template.
5. Describe what you are trying to accomplish and where assistance is needed.

Questions do not need to be framed as technical problems to be legitimate.

A request for clarification, an explanation of system behavior, or help understanding documentation is an appropriate reason to seek support.

### Report a problem

If a project behaves unexpectedly:

1. Open its **Issues** tab.
2. Check whether the problem has already been reported.
3. Select **New issue** and use an available bug-report template.
4. Describe what happened, what you expected, and the circumstances involved.
5. Include relevant error messages, reproduction steps, or supporting evidence when available.

If you cannot reproduce the problem consistently, report what you observed and identify what remains uncertain.

Do not invent missing details or assume a technical cause.

### Request an improvement

Feature requests and improvement proposals may be submitted through repository Issues or Discussions.

Describe the objective, the limitation being addressed, and the change being proposed.

A complete implementation plan is not required. Contributors and users may identify legitimate needs without knowing how to satisfy them technically.

## 3. Accessibility support

Accessibility concerns are functional support concerns, not secondary preferences.

Reports may address barriers involving:

- Screen readers, keyboard navigation, or assistive technologies.
- Visual presentation, contrast, text scaling, or information hierarchy.
- Hearing, speech, mobility, or cognitive accessibility.
- Language clarity and comprehension.
- Authentication, verification, or account recovery.
- Processes that depend unnecessarily on professional knowledge, institutional access, or particular communication methods.

### Requesting assistance

Accessibility issues affecting publicly documented functionality may be reported through repository Issues.

For requests involving personal information, individual circumstances, or sensitive accessibility needs, contact:

**[privacy-contact@mail.meansandmeasures.com](mailto:privacy-contact@mail.meansandmeasures.com)**

You do not need to disclose a diagnosis or provide medical documentation merely to report an accessibility barrier.

Describe the barrier and the intended outcome to the extent you are comfortable and able.

Support should focus on understanding the limitation and identifying an effective means of participation or access.

## 4. Security and confidential concerns

### Security vulnerabilities

Do not disclose exploitable vulnerabilities, credentials, or security-sensitive evidence through public GitHub Issues or Discussions.

Use the affected repository's **Security → Report a vulnerability** feature when enabled.

Alternatively, submit a private report to:

**[privacy-contact@mail.meansandmeasures.com](mailto:privacy-contact@mail.meansandmeasures.com)**

See [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) for reporting procedures, scope, and disclosure guidance.

### Conduct and governance concerns

Confidential conduct concerns, including those involving repository maintainers, may be submitted to the same private reporting address.

See [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) for community standards and reporting procedures, or [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md) for governance-related questions and challenges.

## 5. Information that helps us assist you

Support requests are most useful when they identify:

- **Objective:** What you are trying to accomplish.
- **Context:** The project, feature, or process involved.
- **Observed behavior:** What happened or prevented progress.
- **Expected behavior:** What you reasonably expected to happen.
- **Evidence:** Relevant error messages, screenshots, logs, or documentation.
- **Impact:** How the problem affects use, access, or functionality.

Provide only information that is relevant and available.

Not every question requires logs, technical terminology, or a reproducible test case.

If an issue cannot be described through the standard template, explain it in your own words.

## 6. Support expectations

Support requests are evaluated according to their substance, context, and potential impact.

A support request may result in:

- An explanation or clarification.
- Guidance toward existing functionality or documentation.
- Investigation of a reported issue.
- Identification of a workaround.
- A proposed documentation or implementation change.
- Referral to another appropriate project or support channel.
- A determination that the requested functionality is outside the project's current scope.

Not every request can be resolved immediately or result in a software change.

Means+Measures does not establish universal response-time or resolution guarantees through this document. Project-specific commitments, where adopted, should be documented separately.

A request should not be dismissed solely because the requester lacks technical expertise, institutional standing, or the ability to propose a solution.

## 7. Privacy and responsible information sharing

Public GitHub Issues and Discussions are accessible to others and may be indexed or retained outside the repository.

Do not include passwords, authentication tokens, private keys, sensitive personal information, or confidential records in public support requests.

When investigating a problem requires sensitive details, use the designated private reporting address rather than publishing that information.

Provide the minimum information necessary to explain the issue. Logs and screenshots should be reviewed for sensitive material before sharing.

## 8. Contributing improvements

Support requests can reveal limitations that existing development processes or documentation have not identified.

People who encounter those limitations are not required to implement the solution themselves.

Those interested in contributing fixes, documentation, testing, design improvements, or other changes can consult [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

Feedback and contributions should be evaluated on their demonstrated relevance and merits, not the contributor's credentials or institutional position.

## Our approach

**Equity of means:** Lack of privilege is not lack of access.

**Equality of measures:** Impacts are judged on their own merits, not the standing of the institution.

Institutions bear responsibility down through systems for making participation accessible.

Authority rises up through people who question, evaluate, and hold those institutions accountable.

Support is one of the ways those responsibilities become operational. The measure of effective support is whether it helps people understand, access, and meaningfully use the systems intended to serve them.
