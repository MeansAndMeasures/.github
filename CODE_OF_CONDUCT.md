# Code of Conduct

**Means+Measures**

**Equity of means. Equality of measures.**

Means+Measures is committed to an environment in which people can participate, contribute, question decisions, and challenge established practices without their access or standing being determined by privilege, credentials, or institutional affiliation.

Our community standards protect both meaningful participation and substantive disagreement. Respect does not require agreement, and accountability does not require deference.

## 1. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to participation in Means+Measures repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and other project-managed collaboration spaces.

It also applies to conduct undertaken in an official project capacity or represented as being on behalf of Means+Measures.

This document governs participation in project spaces. It does not claim authority over unrelated private conduct or establish organizational employment policies.

## 2. Expected conduct

Participants are expected to:

- **Engage on substance.** Evaluate ideas, implementations, evidence, and outcomes rather than relying on personal standing or institutional authority.
- **Respect individual dignity.** Treat others as legitimate participants regardless of their background, identity, credentials, experience, or communication style.
- **Make participation accessible.** Communicate clearly, identify relevant context, and avoid introducing unnecessary procedural or technical barriers.
- **Challenge constructively.** Question assumptions, identify errors, and disagree directly without personal intimidation or harassment.
- **Represent evidence accurately.** Distinguish observations, established facts, interpretations, and proposals.
- **Accept scrutiny.** Be willing to explain decisions, examine contrary evidence, and correct demonstrated errors.
- **Respect privacy.** Handle personal information, confidential material, and security-sensitive findings appropriately.
- **Exercise authority accountably.** Use administrative and moderation permissions for legitimate project purposes, with decisions grounded in identifiable conduct and applicable standards.

Participants are not required to adopt a particular communication style, express agreement, or avoid justified criticism to be considered respectful.

## 3. Unacceptable conduct

The following conduct is incompatible with participation in Means+Measures spaces:

- Harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or targeted abuse.
- Discrimination or targeted hostility based on protected or personal characteristics.
- Repeated unwanted personal contact after a clear request to stop.
- Deliberate exposure of private, identifying, or confidential information without authorization.
- Knowingly false accusations, fabricated evidence, or deliberate misrepresentation intended to harm others.
- Attempts to silence, exclude, or retaliate against participants for raising legitimate concerns, providing evidence, or questioning decisions.
- Sustained disruption that materially prevents others from participating or project work from proceeding.
- Abuse of repository permissions, moderation tools, or institutional standing to evade scrutiny or impose arbitrary outcomes.
- Disclosure of security vulnerabilities or exploit details in public project spaces when an appropriate private reporting process is available.

This list is illustrative rather than exhaustive. Conduct should be evaluated in context, with attention to evidence, severity, repetition, and actual or reasonably foreseeable impact.

## 4. Disagreement, criticism, and accountability

**Disagreement is not misconduct.**

Participants may criticize decisions, challenge maintainers, question institutional practices, and identify failures in project design or implementation.

Direct language, frustration, unpopular positions, and persistent evidence-based questioning do not independently constitute harassment or disruption.

The distinction is between challenging a position or action and engaging in conduct that targets, threatens, or improperly obstructs people.

A participant's tone may be relevant to understanding an interaction, but should not substitute for evaluating its substance or the conduct at issue.

Likewise, claims of good intent do not automatically excuse harmful behavior, and claims of harm do not automatically establish that misconduct occurred.

Moderation must not become a mechanism for insulating institutional decisions from legitimate examination.

## 5. Accessibility and equitable participation

Participation includes the ability to understand project information, communicate concerns, and engage with decisions through reasonably accessible processes.

Contributors may have different communication needs, technical resources, language proficiency, disabilities, or levels of familiarity with GitHub and project conventions.

Where practical, maintainers should support accessible alternatives and clarify procedures without making participation contingent on unnecessary demonstrations of expertise.

A request for clarification or an accessible participation method should not be treated as evidence that a contributor's substantive position lacks merit.

Equity of means requires examining whether the process itself creates avoidable barriers, not merely whether identical procedures are applied to everyone.

## 6. Reporting conduct concerns

Concerns about conduct within Means+Measures project spaces may be reported privately to:

**privacy-contact@mail.meansandmeasures.com**

This is the designated contact for confidential conduct reports, sensitive concerns, and matters involving repository maintainers or moderators.

### Encrypted reporting

The reporting mailbox supports OpenPGP encryption. Reporters may encrypt correspondence using the mailbox's public key.

**Public-key fingerprint (OpenPGP):**

The public key is published as [`PGP_PUBLIC_KEY.asc`](PGP_PUBLIC_KEY.asc).

Verify the key's fingerprint before encrypting sensitive information. Encryption is optional and is not required to submit a report.

PGP encryption protects message contents when used correctly, but does not necessarily conceal email metadata, including sender and recipient addresses.

### Public project discussions

For concerns that do not involve sensitive personal information or confidential allegations, a repository issue or discussion may be appropriate.

Do not publish private allegations, personal information, or sensitive supporting evidence solely to initiate a report.

Security vulnerabilities should follow the separate [Security Policy](SECURITY.md) and its private vulnerability reporting process.

### Information useful in a report

Where available and appropriate, include:

- The conduct or incident being reported.
- When and where it occurred.
- Relevant links, messages, or other supporting evidence.
- Individuals or project spaces involved.
- Any immediate participation or safety concern.
- Whether the matter has previously been raised.

Reports should describe observable conduct as precisely as possible and distinguish firsthand observations from secondhand accounts or interpretations.

A report does not require the reporter to assign a disciplinary category or propose a sanction.

## 7. Review and response

Conduct concerns should receive an assessment proportionate to their seriousness, available evidence, and effects on participation.

Review should consider:

1. **Relevant facts:** What conduct is documented, disputed, or not yet established?
2. **Context:** What circumstances materially affect interpretation?
3. **Applicable standards:** Which provisions, if any, are implicated?
4. **Impact:** What consequences occurred, and what further effects are reasonably foreseeable?
5. **Proportionality:** What response, if any, is justified by the evidence?
6. **Consistency:** How have materially similar circumstances been evaluated?

Where practical, individuals whose conduct is under review should have an opportunity to provide relevant context before a consequential determination is finalized.

Immediate protective measures may be appropriate when there is a credible risk of serious harm, exposure of private information, or ongoing disruption.

Such measures should be distinguished from final findings of misconduct.

## 8. Moderation and corrective measures

Depending on the circumstances, responses may include:

- Clarification of expectations or applicable project procedures.
- Requests to modify or discontinue specific conduct.
- Mediation or facilitated discussion, where appropriate and mutually acceptable.
- Removal of content that violates project standards.
- Temporary restrictions on participation.
- Revocation of repository or community access.
- Referral to another competent authority where the matter exceeds project governance.

Responses should be proportionate to the conduct and its consequences.

A disagreement about policy, architecture, evidence, or maintainer judgment is not, by itself, grounds for disciplinary action.

Moderation should address demonstrated conduct rather than requiring conformity of opinion or personal deference.

## 9. Conflicts of interest and review of authority

Individuals exercising moderation authority should identify material conflicts of interest that could compromise their ability to assess a concern fairly.

Concerns involving a repository maintainer or moderator may be submitted directly to the designated private reporting address rather than through that individual's repository.

When the conduct of a moderator or maintainer is itself at issue, review by another appropriately authorized person is preferable where available.

No participant should be considered immune from review solely because of administrative permissions, project ownership, professional standing, or institutional position.

This document does not establish an independent appeals body or assign authority to individuals who have not been authorized to exercise it.

Concerns about governance decisions may be raised through the processes described in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md).

## 10. Privacy, confidentiality, and transparency

Reports involving personal information or sensitive allegations should be handled with appropriate discretion.

Information should be shared only as necessary for assessment, response, and applicable obligations.

Confidentiality does not mean that every aspect of a decision must remain unexplained. Where practical, project maintainers should communicate the applicable standard, general reasoning, and resulting action without unnecessarily exposing private information.

Absolute confidentiality or anonymity cannot be guaranteed through this document.

## 11. Retaliation

Retaliation against a participant for making a good-faith report, presenting evidence, requesting accessibility, or challenging a decision is unacceptable.

This protection does not prevent proportionate responses to independently established misconduct.

Disagreement with a report, or a determination that its allegations are unsupported, does not alone establish that the report was made in bad faith.

## 12. Policy interpretation and revision

This Code of Conduct should be interpreted consistently with the Means+Measures [Governance Policy](GOVERNANCE.md) and [Contribution Guidelines](CONTRIBUTING.md).

Proposed changes may be submitted through the established contribution process.

Revisions should identify the problem being addressed, explain consequential changes, and preserve sufficient context for participants to understand how community expectations have evolved.

## Governing commitment

**Equity of means ensures that people can participate without privilege being the price of admission. Equality of measures ensures that contributions, conduct, and institutional actions are judged on their merits.**

Institutions bear responsibility down through systems for making participation accessible.

Authority rises up through people who question, evaluate, and hold those institutions accountable.

These standards apply to Means+Measures itself.
